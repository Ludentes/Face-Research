Providers’ full pipelines

Response latency: About 0.6s less waiting for each reply.

Response latency is the time from the user finishing speaking to the avatar starting its reply, measured from audio recordings captured in the rater’s browser in the same way for every provider. Anam’s median was 1.24s, compared with 1.82s for HeyGen, the next quickest provider.

This is end-to-end latency as experienced by the user, including transcription, language-model processing, speech and avatar generation, and network time between the rater’s machine and the provider’s server. Raters were geographically varied, primarily across Europe and North America.

Median is the middle measured time. P90 and P95 are the times at or below which 90% and 95% of replies fell.

Connection time: Anam’s P95 was quicker than every rival’s median.

Connection time, or time to first frame (TTFF), runs from pressing Start to the first visible avatar video frame, including the provider’s session-creation API call. Anam’s median was 1.42s, about 2.2s quicker than HeyGen at 3.66s.

P95 and P99 are the times at or below which 95% and 99% of connections completed. Counts below are measured connections, rather than conversation turns.

How do the avatar models compare on the same pipeline?

In this separate study, 60 people held 1,200 rated conversations with the same speech-to-text model, language model and text-to-speech model across providers. Only the avatar provider changed.

The shared pipeline used Deepgram Nova-3 as the speech-to-text model, Groq-hosted GPT OSS 120B as the language model and Cartesia Sonic-3.6 as the text-to-speech model.

Synthesia is included here because it uses a customer-supplied speech-and-language pipeline. This is a like-for-like comparison of that setup. The full-pipeline and LiveKit studies are reported separately because their configurations differ.

Methodology: How response latency and connection time were measured

The main benchmark used 60 participants and 960 rated conversations, with each provider running its full pipeline. Anam used Cara-4.1 Express; competitors ran their defaults. The separate LiveKit study used 60 participants and 1,200 rated conversations on a shared speech-and-language pipeline.

Response latency

Time from the user finishing speaking to the avatar starting its reply. Measured from the two audio channels of each browser-captured recording, using the same method for every provider.

Connection time

Time from pressing Start to the first visible avatar video frame, also called time to first frame (TTFF). Measured from client connection events, including the provider’s session-creation API call.

Median and percentiles

The median is the middle measured time. P90, P95 and P99 describe the slower end of the results: 90%, 95% and 99% of measurements fall at or below those times.

The full-pipeline results include 4,703 measured turns and 926 measured connections; the LiveKit results include 5,942 turns and 1,211 connections. A conversation can contain several turns. Counts reflect the measurements available for each metric and are separate from the number of rated conversations.

Response tables show the median, P90, P95 and the share of replies taking over three seconds. Connection tables show the median, P95 and P99. The two studies are presented separately because their pipeline configurations differ.

Measured on October 16, 2026. These results describe the configurations tested on that date. Products and performance can change.

Data and transparency

The benchmark repository includes the methodology, evaluation code, de-identified ratings, session telemetry and provider configurations, so you can inspect the setup and reproduce the results.

Anam conducted this benchmark, and we recognise the conflict of interest in comparing our own product with competitors. The figures above use the study report’s published precision. Full-pipeline and LiveKit results are presented separately so each comparison reflects the configuration tested.
