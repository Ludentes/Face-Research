What is Hedra Character 3?
Character 3
is Hedra's audio-driven character-video model. Its current model page lists a start image and required audio, with 540p, 720p, and 1080p output options. The older statement that Character 3 is limited to 720p is no longer consistent with that page.
Use it for a speaking or singing character when the source is a still image. Confirm the settings available in your account rather than assuming that every model on the platform has the same input, duration, or output limits.
Character 3 versus Hedra Omnia
Character 3
is Hedra's long-form audio-driven portrait model. It requires a start frame and audio, supports 540p, 720p and 1080p, and accepts up to 600 seconds of audio on the current API route. Start here when the deliverable is a presenter, singer, podcast clip or other speech-driven character performance.
Hedra Omnia
launched in February 2026 as Hedra's more advanced model for character dialogue in dynamic environments with camera control. Evaluate it when the scene needs more than a largely portrait-led performance. Hedra currently presents Omnia and Character 3 as separate choices, so record the selected model instead of calling every Hedra output “Character 3.”

Do Hedra credits roll over after renewal or cancellation?
Subscription credits reset each billing cycle; separately purchased credit packs do not expire.
Hedra’s
pricing FAQ
says the monthly allowance is spent before pack credits. Size a recurring plan around work you expect to complete that cycle, rather than treating unused monthly credits as savings for a later project.
An unexpired balance does not guarantee continued access after cancelling. The same FAQ says most functionality becomes unavailable apart from limited watermarked generation, and you must resubscribe to spend remaining non-expiring credits. Before buying a large pack for occasional projects, account for the subscription needed when you return.
Character 3 API cost per finished attempt
The current
Character 3 API page
lists $0.025 per second at 540p, $0.05 at 720p, and $0.0625 at 1080p. A 60-second attempt is therefore $1.50, $3.00, or $3.75 before retries. A ten-minute 1080p attempt is $37.50 before retries. These are API rates for this model, not a conversion from Hedra subscription credits.
Budget by accepted output. If three one-minute 720p attempts are needed to approve one clip, the generation cost is $9 before any voice, editing, storage or review work. Save the model, resolution, audio duration, attempt count and actual charge with each result.
How to create the first talking video
1. Open Hedra and check the selected workflow
Sign in through
Hedra
, open Creative Studio, and choose the talking-character workflow and model. Interface labels can change; the required assets are the useful anchor: a starting image and an approved audio track.
2. Prepare one clear portrait
Use a well-lit face with unobstructed eyes and mouth. Leave room for movement around the head and chin. If you create a character image first, save the approved image instead of repeatedly prompting for an approximate replacement.
3. Approve the audio before animation
Record your line or generate speech, then listen to it by itself. Correct pronunciation, pauses, and the message before using it as an input. Hedra's
photo-to-video guide
describes this image-and-audio workflow.
Check the Character 3 API input limits
For the
current Character 3 API route
, the starting image must be no larger than 10.4 MB. Each audio file must run between 0.5 and 600 seconds and stay within 104.8 MB. These are API limits; check Studio separately.
For a thirteen-minute narration, plan separate clips of ten minutes and three minutes or shorter editorial segments. Reuse the approved portrait, cut at complete sentences, and assemble the returned clips in an editor. Do not truncate the audio merely to fit one request. This is a planning workflow, not a tested Hedra output.
4. Check the model, resolution, and charge
Choose settings appropriate to the deliverable and review the quoted credit use. Begin with a short segment that contains the expressions and pacing you need. A low-cost preview can screen the inputs, but it does not establish the quality of a different final mode.
5. Generate, review, and download
Watch the whole output at normal speed. Inspect the mouth at pauses, the eyes during movement, and the face shape through the sentence. Confirm the downloaded file's dimensions and export conditions before making a series.
Control which character speaks and how long the clip runs
For API projects with multiple people in the image, map each audio track to the intended speaker. The
Character 3 API reference
accepts up to four audio files. A single [x, y] point selects the speaker when one voice is supplied; multiple voices require one point per audio file, in the same order. These are API controls, not a promise that every Studio screen exposes them.
The optional duration uses milliseconds. Leave it unset to follow the supplied audio; setting it trims the audio or adds silence to reach that length. For example, an 18-second recording with duration 20,000 adds two seconds of silence; duration 12,000 cuts six seconds. These illustrate the documented rule, not tested outputs. Omit the override when you want the complete recording unchanged.
A sample brief for a product explainer
Use a licensed or original presenter image and a short line such as: “Here's how the cap opens. Turn it once, then lift.” Time the actual recording and make the instruction match the real product.
Pair the presenter with footage of the action. The talking character introduces the demonstration; it should not invent evidence that the product works. Do not present a generated actor as a real customer describing a purchase they never made.
Keep these three assets together: the approved image, the final voice recording, and the visible demonstration. When the instructions change, update the relevant asset and review the combined video. For longer structures, use the
product video script templates
.
Estimate the cost of a series
Suppose you need ten clips and each attempt receives a quote of Q credits. One attempt per clip costs 10Q; two attempts per clip cost 20Q. If only eight of those clips are usable, the cost per usable clip is the total spent divided by eight.
This is planning arithmetic, not a Hedra rate or an observed success rate. Record actual charges and usable outputs in your project. A subscription price alone does not show the cost of a finished campaign.

Frequently asked questions
Is Hedra free, and do free videos have a watermark?
Hedra advertises a free start, but its public pricing page does not promise one fixed recurring free-credit allowance. Check your trial balance, eligible model, watermark, download and commercial-use conditions in the account. Paid pricing cards list commercial use; do not assume that establishes the same conditions for the free route.
Does Character 3 support 1080p?
Yes, the current Character 3 model page lists 1080p alongside 540p and 720p. Check availability for the selected workflow and account; this does not establish the same limit for every Hedra model.
Is a generated avatar clip the same as a live avatar?
No. A rendered clip is a finished video. An interactive avatar also needs a streaming, voice, and response system. Confirm the current developer product and its billing separately; do not use an old streaming rate to budget ordinary video generations.
Can Magic Hour make a talking video from a photo?
Yes.
Magic Hour Talking Photo
takes an image and audio directly. Magic Hour also offers text-to-video, image-to-video, and editing workflows. It is inaccurate to describe the platform as only transforming existing footage.
Should I pay before trying my character?
Evaluate the available sample first. Choose a paid tier when the result fits your project and you need its credits or export conditions. Confirm that your image, voice, and script can be used for the intended purpose.
